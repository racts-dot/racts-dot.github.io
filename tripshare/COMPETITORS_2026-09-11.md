# What the other trip planners hand over — read 11 September 2026

The product bar's leg 2 asks for a list against a list: open the competitors,
write down what each one GIVES a traveller, then say what this gives that they
do not. Not adjectives. This had never been done for TripShare.

Read on 11 Sep 2026 from each product's own pages and current pricing write-ups.
Prices are US dollars as published that day. **Re-read before quoting these.**

## What each one hands over

| | Account needed | Costs | What you get for nothing | What you must pay for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wanderlog** | Yes | Free, or **$39.99/yr** | Unlimited places, maps, live collaboration, booking import | Offline access, AI suggestions (free tier is **5 AI messages total**), route optimisation, Google Maps export |
| **TripIt** | Yes | Free, or **$49/yr** Pro | Itinerary built from forwarded confirmation emails | Flight alerts, seat and fare tracking, airport maps |
| **Tripsy** | Yes | Paid | — | Email parsing, calendar sync, flight alerts, expenses, documents, travel stats. **Apple-first** |
| **TripShare** | **No** | **Free, no tier** | Every feature it has | Nothing |

## Where TripShare genuinely wins

- **No account, no sign-up, no email.** Every product above needs one. TripShare
  opens and works.
- **Nothing leaves the device.** The trips live in the browser. The others are
  cloud services by design.
- **No paid tier at all**, so no feature is dangled and withheld.
- **Offline by default.** Wanderlog charges $39.99/yr for offline access;
  TripShare has no other mode.
- **Prints and shares as a link**, no app install at either end.

## Where TripShare genuinely loses — said plainly

- **No flight alerts, no email parsing, no live prices, no maps.** Every rival
  above has at least two of those. This is the biggest gap and it is not close.
- **Nothing syncs.** Lose the device, lose the trip, unless a backup was taken.
- **No collaboration.** Wanderlog gives live shared editing away for free.

## ⛔ What this means for the AI planner specifically

**The planner is the only part of TripShare that undoes its own advantage.**
The measured differentiator above is *no account, nothing leaves the device*.
The planner sends the traveller's description and dates to an outside service.
It is still the smallest possible version of that — one box, opt-in, off by
default, and nothing else in the trip is sent — but it is a real trade of the
one thing this app has that the paid products do not.

⚠ **And on the feature itself it does not lead.** Wanderlog's free tier gives 5
AI messages and its paid tier gives unlimited AI *with a live places database
behind it*. TripShare's planner has no map, no opening hours, no availability.
It cannot beat that on suggestion quality and should not claim to. What it can
honestly claim is: **no account, no sign-up, and a plan you tick before anything
is written.** That is the only ground on which this feature wins.

**Leg 2 verdict: it does not hand over more than the competitors on planning
features, and it is not trying to. It hands over more on privacy and access.
Whether that is worth turning on is Rachel's call, not a gate's.**
