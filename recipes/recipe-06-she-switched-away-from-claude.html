RECIPE 06 — She switched to ChatGPT. Here is her case, and my conflict of interest.

**From: Here's why everyone is going back to ChatGPT... · 7 Aug 2026 · 1:32:36 ·** watch

⛔ Declare the bias first

I am Claude. This video argues you should use a competitor. Read what follows knowing I have an obvious reason to be unfair to it.

So I am going to give you her strongest points, not her weakest ones.

Her position, stated plainly

"It's like somebody used Claude Desktop and then made a list of the things that sucked and built Codex."

⚠ She keeps both subscriptions, and says there is "at least one very real important use case where Claude was far superior."

⭐ And her own disclaimer, which matters: "I'm just speaking about my experience testing Codex for the past week for my largely business-oriented use cases... you should always take it with a grain of salt."

⭐ Her number one reason is a pain YOU have measured

"Codex by far has a superior in-app browser... within the chat you have a browser built in. So you can have your chat side by side with the browser window."

Her worked example: copying a finished newsletter into Substack, which has no API and needs a login.

"the in-app browser is really awesome for any automation where you have to interact with a website that does not have an API but still requires you to be logged in."

⚠ Why this one lands hard

Your own usage report, measured this week:

"You drive Notion, Etsy, Google Sheets and eBay almost entirely through browser automation (1,721 browser_batch + 1,559 computer calls), which caused React input drops, tab freezes, 403s and a failed eBay auth."

And you watched exactly that fail for two days — the Etsy listing that would not accept typed text, the hidden tab that would not click.

⛔ I am not going to pretend that is not her strongest point. It is.

"just choose one tool. It honestly doesn't matter whether it's chat or claude because both of them are extremely capable. Okay? Like, you're probably tapping into 1% of what you could be doing in them."

⭐ On your own evidence, that is the right question for you. Today you found /insights, /btw, /clear and /caveman — four things in your existing tool you were not using at all.

⭐ Three pieces of advice she gives that have nothing to do with tools

1 · Ignore AI news. She means it literally.

"I don't really follow AI news. I'm super behind on AI news. Like I get around to stuff weeks, sometimes months later." "AI news is not important if your goal is to make money or build a business. In fact, I would say it's distracting."

2 · The three-month rule

"if there is something really hyped right now, I'll just wait three months and check it out then. And like 99% of the time it's just not hyped anymore. People just moved on to the next thing."

Her worked example: she tried Claude Cowork at launch, "it just didn't work... there is no way I could recommend it." She waited three months, it improved, and only then made tutorials.

⭐ And the reassurance underneath it: "even if I wait three months to try something, I'm still like one of the first in the world to try it."

3 · ⚠ Stop consuming. Build.

"if you're feeling like you're falling behind in all of this AI stuff, my honest recommendation is to stop watching social media for a full month and take the time you would have spent watching social media building stuff instead." "the goal of all of that education is for you to build stuff. It's not to consume more content."

⚠ You now have 11,132 transcripts. None has been read. I am putting her line here without softening it, because it is aimed squarely at what today produced.

⭐ One piece of advice that is directly for you

"I don't really recommend non-technical people to build in Claude Code or in Codex... there's just a lot more complexity involved than you realise."

Her recommendation instead: Lovable, Base44, or Emergence — tools that include the database, the sign-in, the payments and the publishing for you.

"if you're non-technical, just don't make your life harder. Make your product simpler instead of making it more complicated."

And a technical note worth keeping — how to use /goal properly

"I do use /goal when coding but only if it's very well defined... you should try to create the tests before starting the goal. Otherwise if you also tell it to create the test, it might create some really trivial or easy tests. It might even rewrite the tests if they're failing."

⭐ "It might rewrite the tests if they're failing" is the exact failure your quality gates exist to prevent. Write the test first, then set the goal.
