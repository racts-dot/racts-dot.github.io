The six agents — what each one is for

Her framing: you stop asking questions and start handing over tasks.

"We're used to going back and forth. It's like a partner... But nowadays we're moving into the world of agents. You're just giving it a task to do."
AgentWhat it does
FacilitatorSits in your meeting. Takes the notes, pulls the action items, schedules the follow-ups. "You don't need" a separate note-taker
ResearcherDigs through your files and the web
Analyst"every employee has access to an expert agent able to perform complex multi-step data analysis" — she calls it "analyst level work... a step up"
Prompt coach⭐ "the most underrated agent that there is" — you tell it what you are trying to do and it writes the prompt
Idea coachBrainstorming
Writing / visualDrafting and images

⭐ The prompt coach is the one worth knowing about even if you never use Copilot — the idea that you describe the goal and something else writes the prompt.

⭐ Four features worth stealing, whatever tool you use

1 · Temporary chat — ignore your own standing instructions, just this once

"Maybe you have your custom instructions that you wanted to break things down very simply, but for this one time you don't want that... temporary chat will literally ignore your custom instructions. A lot of people don't even know that exists."

⭐ You have 62 standing rules that apply to every reply. There is no way to say "not this time" without arguing with them. That is a genuinely good idea and you do not have it.

2 · Scheduled prompts

"you could say over the weekend, take all the emails that I've received and put them in order to what's most important."

Save a prompt once, have it run Monday morning.

3 · The prompt gallery

"How many people have said 'I don't know how to write a prompt'... all you have to do is drop down here and you're going to see all these prompts. And these prompts are going to actually pull from your work."

Her example: "Help me prepare for a one-on-one" pulls every relevant Word file, meeting and Teams message. "Instead of spending 30 minutes... I can prepare for a meeting within like 5 seconds."

4 · A work/web toggle

One switch: answer from my company's files, or answer from the internet. ⭐ Simple, and most tools make you say it in words every time.

⚠ Two things in here that are quietly interesting

Copilot is other people's models wearing a Microsoft badge

"The GPT models are right here. So you can get either GPT 5.2 think deeper or GPT 5.2 quick response. Co-Pilot is built on the back of that and most people don't even understand that. And for the agents... those are actually built on the back end of Claude."

⭐ So "which AI is best" matters less than you would think — the same models are inside several products you might be choosing between.

Her model-setting advice, which is sensible anywhere

"I would advise them to just have it default to auto... if you think it's taking too long then go to quick response, or if you think the responses aren't good enough, then go to think deeper."

Do not pick the most powerful setting by default. Pick it when the answer disappoints you.
