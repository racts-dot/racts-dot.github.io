# RECIPE 17 — Free slide decks with NotebookLM

From: [How I created these VIRAL POWERPOINTS (free with ai)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA) · 13 Dec 2025 · 11:38 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA)

**You give NotebookLM some sources and a design prompt. It builds a slide deck from those sources in a few minutes, for free. The prompt mostly changes the look. Only the beginner prompt is read out, and even that one stops before its theme.**

# ⭐⭐ THE ONE THING — the sources decide what the deck says

A notebook is a workspace. You add sources first, and the deck is built from them.

> "Think of sources as the information that notebook LM is going to use to craft your PowerPoint presentation." ([01:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=90s))

Sources can be a YouTube link, a website, a Google Drive file or pasted text ([01:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=90s)). She also mixes in audio files ([03:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=211s)). There is a web search to find more ([02:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=121s)).

```
SOURCES            PROMPT               SLIDE DECK PANEL
───────            ──────               ────────────────
YouTube link       who it is and        pencil icon
website       ──►  how it should   ──►  length: short
Drive file         look                 paste prompt
pasted text        (+ your outline)     generate, a few minutes
```

Her last point is the one to keep:

> "viral presentations are not necessarily visually loud. They are intellectually loud" ([11:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=660s))

## Why this matters to you

You already have written research: rankings, buyer notes, what each binder contains. That is exactly the kind of text a notebook takes as a source. The tool turns it into something you can look at in pages, not paragraphs.

⭐ The design prompt is not where the facts come from. If the source is thin, the deck will be thin, however good the prompt.

# The steps she shows

1. Go to NotebookLM and click "try Notebook LM". She says to sign up "completely for free. Just use a personal Gmail account." ([01:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=61s))
2. Click "create new notebook" ([01:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=61s)).
3. Add a source. Her demo used one of her own YouTube videos: click YouTube, paste the link, click insert ([01:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=90s)).
4. On the right, find the slide deck button and click its pencil icon ([02:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=151s)).
5. The default is a detailed deck. She picks a shorter length so it generates faster ([02:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=151s)).
6. Paste the prompt into "describe the slide deck you want to create". You can add your own outline there too, but "this is optional" ([03:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=180s)).
7. Click generate. It takes "a couple minutes" per deck, and you can start the next one while it runs ([03:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=211s)).

When it is done, the preview has buttons to rename, share, show the prompt used, download as a PDF, and start a slideshow ([07:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=451s)).

# The three prompt levels — what is actually shown

## Beginner — read out, but not all of it

The words she speaks at [02:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=151s) are:

> "You are a top.1% PowerPoint presentation designer using only the provided sources. Create a visually stunning viral slide deck"

She then lists the principles: "Visuals first, one main idea per slide, minimal text, and here's the theme."

⚠ That is the caption as heard. "top.1%" is probably "top 0.1%". Her theme text is on screen but never spoken, so it is not in the transcript. Do not treat the quote above as the full prompt.

## Intermediate — described, not read

She says it adds guidance on visuals, "particularly one strong visual metaphor for each slide", plus a narrative and story arc ([04:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=241s)).

## Advanced — described, not read

She says it builds on the intermediate one and adds ([05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=300s), [05:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=331s), [06:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=361s)):

- a longer list of "viral visual layouts"
- turning data into "clean, elegant visualizations"
- a content order: a strong start, the context, the central concept, key components
- typography and theme rules you can change to your own colours
- safety rules at the bottom

The safety rules tell it to use only ideas "explicitly present in the sources". She says this "helps reduce hallucinations" ([06:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=390s)).

⛔ The full intermediate and advanced prompts are not in this recipe. She points to two places for them:

- her newsletter, linked in the video description ([04:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=241s))
- her shared notebook, which she calls "publicly available" ([11:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=660s)), and where you can "copy paste the prompts directly" ([07:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=420s))

Her other route is to screenshot the prompt from the video and have ChatGPT type it out ([04:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSUMR8sAhOA&t=241s)). ⚠ That is a copy made by a model reading an image. Check it word for word before you rely on it.

# ⚠ Before any of this goes near a listing

- The deck is made of AI graphics, not your pages. Nothing in the video puts a real product page into a slide. A listing image must show what the buyer actually gets. A slide with an invented binder picture would misrepresent the product.
- Safer uses first. A private deck of your own research. A buyer guide slide that explains, in words, what is inside a binder, checked line by line against the real PDF.
- AI disclosure on Etsy. The video says nothing about it. ⚠ Check Etsy's current rules yourself before any NotebookLM slide goes into a listing or a download.
- Your rule on artwork. It bans code-drawn art as product art. These are AI-made graphics, a different thing, but the same worry applies: nobody checked what the picture shows.
- Facts can still drift. Even the advanced prompt only "helps reduce" hallucinations. Read every number on every slide against its source.
- Sharing is public. She shares her notebook with a public link. ⛔ Sharing yours, or posting a deck, is a public step and needs your yes.
- Price. The only price in the video is "free", as of 13 Dec 2025. Uploading your notes also puts them in a Google account.

# What to try — one step, tonight

This costs nothing and makes nothing public.

```
1. open NotebookLM with your personal Gmail and
   click "create new notebook"

2. add ONE source: paste in the text of one of
   your own research notes (a copy)

3. slide deck, pencil icon, length: short

4. paste her spoken beginner prompt (the caption
   says "top.1%"; 0.1% is my reading):
   "You are a top 0.1% PowerPoint presentation
   designer using only the provided sources.
   Create a visually stunning viral slide deck
   by following these design principles. Visuals
   first, one main idea per slide, minimal text."
   then add one line of theme in your own words,
   e.g. your shop colours (her theme is not in
   the transcript)

5. generate, then open it and check every fact
   on every slide against your note
```

Step 5 is the point. Count how many slides say something your note does not. That number tells you whether this is safe to use for buyers.
