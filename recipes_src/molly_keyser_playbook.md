# Molly Keyser's method, Etsy included

Written 14 Sep 2026 for Rachel. **It is Keyser's method as the 56 Etsy-related video summaries show
it, not all 340 videos**, so it cannot claim to be the whole method. Every rule cites the videos it
came from as V1 to V56; the list at the end links each one to its Notion summary.

## Read this first: what this is, and what it is not

- **It is Keyser's method as the video summaries state it. It is not proof the method works.**
  The income figures in the video titles are Keyser's own claims, and none were checked.
- **How it was made:** video → a Notion summary written by AI → Gemini 3.7 Flash pulled the steps
  out of each summary, each with a quote → a script kept only the steps whose quote really is in
  the summary (500 of 506) → I read all 500 and wrote this. The quote check proves a step matches
  the summary. It does not prove the summary matches the video.
- **"56 videos"** means the summaries with Etsy in the title or the first 4,000 characters. A video
  that mentions Etsy only further down is not in here.
- **Dates run from 15 Feb 2022 to 6 Jul 2026**, and V7 is undated. Tools and prices named here may
  have changed since. None were re-checked.
- **A list of V numbers shows how often Keyser repeats a point, not that it is true.** Fifty videos
  from one creator saying the same thing are one opinion, repeated.
- **Keyser earns money from the alternative to Etsy.** Part 6 says how.
- **Whose words are whose.** Parts 1 to 5 and 8 are Keyser's advice as the summaries state it; each
  line carries its V numbers. The "carries across" table, Part 6, the questions in Part 7 and
  anything marked "my reading" are my adaptation for this shop.
- **It is a selection, not all 500 steps.** Steps that repeat, or that only matter to a YouTube

- **Every figure given as Keyser's (a line citing V numbers) appears in a cited video's transcript**,

  from the dated files named beside them. The wording of each rule comes from the AI summaries and

## The method in three lines

1. Pick a problem you have solved yourself.
2. Make one small product that fixes it.
3. Sell it from your own page and email list. Etsy is optional, and Part 5 says why Keyser avoids it.

**What carries across to an Etsy shop, and what does not.** This table is my Etsy adaptation, not
Keyser's advice; the review rounds agreed with the split, but none of it is tested on Etsy sales:

| Could carry across | Does not, without Keyser's own audience |
|---|---|
| How to make the product: short, one result, your own method (Part 2; R3 to R6) | What to build: the painful-problem and health, wealth or relationships filters (R1, R2) |
| How to describe it: say the result early (R7), **but keep the search words and a plain list of what is in the file**, because Etsy buyers search and compare contents (Gemini, review 36) | What to charge: Keyser's prices come from a sales page with a warm email list (R8) |
| | Where to sell: the funnel in Part 4 needs a channel and a list the shop does not have |

## Part 1. Pick the problem

- Start from a problem you have solved for yourself or someone else, advice people ask you for, or
  something you enjoy teaching. Use skills and a track record you already have, not a trend. — V1,
  V5, V9, V12, V22, V31, V44, V56
- You only need to know a little more than your buyer, not be the leading expert. — V2, V13, V32
- Pick one specific, painful problem, not a broad topic. Painful problems sell more and carry
  higher prices. — V20, V27, V31, V32, V39, V50
- Aim at one of three areas: health, wealth or relationships. — V17, V23, V26
- Do not invent something original. Pick a topic already proven to sell, and look at what
  competitors offer and charge. — V6, V8, V29
- Talk to the people first: 5 to 10 calls, recorded and transcribed, then have AI pull out their
  struggles and the words they use (V3); or ask them directly about their daily hurdles (V42).
- Look at what already works for rivals: ChatGPT can pick out the recent standout videos from a
  list of competitors in the niche. — V21
- Find the complaint where buyers talk: Facebook groups, Skool, Reddit, and the bad reviews of
  competing products on Amazon or Etsy. — V8, V38
- Once you have an email list, send a short Google Forms survey asking for their single biggest
  struggle. The size Keyser names varies: 200 subscribers (V1, V3, V4, V5, V6, V11); 200 to 300
  (V7); 100 to 200 (V12, V18, V19, V20, V22, V36); 100 (V46).
- Keep the survey short: three questions, on the biggest struggle, the biggest problem and the goal for
  the next 12 months (V15); or 4 to 10 questions (V20).
- Or test with money: pre-sell a live workshop before making it. — V34, V41, V46
- Time-box the choice: an hour at most, and the niche can change later (V9). Or 30 to 60 minutes
  listing 10 to 20 topics, then 10 to 15 minutes picking one (V25).

## Part 2. Make one small product

- First product: an ebook or a 30 to 60 minute workshop. — V15, V19, V20
- Start cheap and short to test the topic; build a course only after that sells. — V42
- Let the problem pick the format (ebook, video, audio, workshop, course or membership), not habit.
  — V36, V51
- Build the audience and the product at the same time, not years of following first. — V55, V56
- Keep it short. The result sets the length: 3 to 30 pages or so (V17); 10 to 50 pages at most (V42); Keyser's
  own examples run 13 pages (V1, V8, V19). Cut the fluff. — V8, V40, V44, V54
- Map it first: where the buyer starts, where they need to end up, and the steps between. Those
  steps become the chapters. — V17, V32, V38, V44
- Draft from bullet points with ChatGPT, then rewrite in your own voice. Train the AI on your own
  past content rather than using generic prompts. — V5, V17, V21, V32, V38
- Put your own framework, stories and experience in. That is what separates it from AI text. — V5
- Lay it out in a free Canva template and export a PDF. — V1, V9, V17, V30, V37, V40
- Other formats Keyser names: templates and checklists for one kind of user and one workflow (V26,
  V40); step-by-step guides from a process you already use (V8, V40, V54); spreadsheet trackers
  (V35, V53); prompt packs, tested before selling (V35, V40); crafting patterns (V53).
- A workshop: outline it in bullet points, run it on Zoom or Google Meet, record it, and sell the
  recording from then on. — V15, V34, V41, V43, V46
- Or pre-sell and build afterwards: a checkout page with the name, what is included, the price and
  a delivery date 2 to 4 weeks out; sell for 5 days (V3) or 72 hours (V11); make it once people
  have paid. — V3, V4, V5, V6, V11
- A free download to collect emails: 1 or 2 pages (V20), such as a checklist, template or script,
  giving a quick win with little reading. A mini ebook of 3 to 5 pages is fine (V21); a full ebook
  or course is too much to give away (V4). — V3, V7, V16, V20, V22

## Part 3. Price it

- Price by what the result is worth to the buyer, not by page count or by competitors. — V8, V38
- Keyser's figures: a simple printable $10, an ebook around $29, a course $297 or more (V37).
  Standard products and templates $9 to $97 (V52). A first product under $100 (V40, V41).
- Keyser's own first ebook is $59 in the newer videos (V2, V12, V23) and $49 in an older one (V37;
  the dates are in the list at the end). Each time it is said to have made over $500,000.
- Courses run $297 to $1,997. — V39, V52
- The summaries do not state a currency. The shop prices in AU$, so compare with care.
- Templates on their own have a low price ceiling and need volume (Keyser's view, not a measured
  rule). — V54
- Work backwards: revenue target divided by price is the number of sales you need. — V36
- Urgency at launch: a price that rises from $59 to $99 (V45, V47); a bonus that ends at midnight
  (V36); a hard close to the sale (V36).
- ⚠ The folder's price rule (`pf/CLAUDE.md`) bans a "was" price, a % off or a strikethrough until
  the item has sold at the higher price. A rising launch price shown as a sale would break it.
- Add checkout add-ons and upsells (V7, V15), but not as a beginner: only after the single product
  sells (V20).
- No guaranteed income or timeline promises in the copy. — V11

## Part 4. Sell it Keyser's way: own page, email list, one channel

- The funnel, in nearly every sales video: content → free download → email list → a checkout page
  for one product. — V2, V3, V8, V10, V16
- One content platform you enjoy, not all of them. — V49, V51
- Keyser's own platform is long-form YouTube, at least once a week. — V4, V24, V34
- Mention the free download 1 to 3 times per video, and link it in the description and a pinned
  comment. — V19, V28
- Build an email list you own and can export. Keyser says email converts 40 times better than
  social media, and gives no source for it. — V2, V8, V9, V10, V17, V28, V32, V39, V49
- Welcome emails: one a day for 5 days, story and tips first, the pitch on days 4 and 5, then
  weekly. — V16, V28, V30, V31
- After that: 30 days of helpful emails, one a week (V28), with a soft pitch in the P.S., then 5
  days of selling, one email a day, repeated. — V28, V33
- A launch: 3 days and 7 emails (V20); or a weekend, 2 emails then 3 (V36); or 3 emails over 3 days
  for a workshop under $100 (V15). Put the checkout link in every email. — V20
- Offer the product on the thank-you page, straight after someone downloads the freebie. — V45
- A checkout page, not a long sales page: product name, who it is for, the result, what is
  included, the price, a payment form and instant delivery. — V11, V18, V21, V24
- Tools: Systeme.io, which Keyser says is free up to 2,000 subscribers (V9, V17, V27, V37); Google
  Drive for the files (V9, V17); your own domain, for trust, at $10 to $30 (V10).
- ⚠ Every video here that recommends buying a domain is sponsored by a domain seller: .online (V2,
  V4, V14) and .store (V10, V13). Read the transcripts.
- An Instagram version: 5 Reels a week, 7 seconds or less, a common misconception then proof, and
  a comment keyword that triggers an automatic message through ManyChat at $10 to $30 a month.
  Say it is a bot. — V47
- Put buyers' testimonials on the checkout page as they come in. — V7, V40
- Clear inactive subscribers out of the list every quarter. — V12
- Growing: once one product sells, send more traffic to it and improve its checkout, rather than
  making more products. — V6, V11, V23, V24

## Part 5. Etsy: what Keyser says

**47 of the 56 videos picked by this rule carry at least one warning about Etsy or marketplaces
like it**, a count for this selection, not for all of Keyser's videos (counted by
`check_playbook.py` from the warning steps; the handmade-goods and trade-off videos below were
picked out by hand). In 4 of
those 47 the warning is about handmade or physical goods (V13, V51, V52, V56), and in 1 it is the
trade-off line below (V54). That leaves **42 videos warning against selling digital products there.**

The reasons, most repeated first:

1. **Fees** *(a real risk; the amounts were not checked)*. Keyser says: a fee for every listing, again when a listing expires or is relisted,
   plus a cut of each sale. — V4, V9, V10, V18, V31, V43, V49. Etsy's current fees for an
   Australian shop were not read for this page; check them before pricing anything.
2. **Paying to be seen** *(Keyser's claim)*. Keyser argues Etsy is so crowded that sellers are pushed into buying Etsy
   Ads, which eat the margin, and that marketplaces cut free reach to sell ads. — V7, V8, V10, V18,
   V33, V49. That is Keyser's claim, not a measured rule; Part 6 has what this shop measured.
3. **Price wars** *(Keyser's claim)*. Competition comes down to who is cheapest (V18, V27, V43). Keyser says there are
   digital products on Etsy "for like 30" and never says 30 what; the AI summary made it cents. — V43
4. **You do not own it.** Etsy can close a shop or change fees overnight, and keeps the customer
   list. — V7, V10, V15, V20, V22, V27, V28, V36, V40, V49. On shutdowns, V7's transcript says
   Keyser has heard "a lot of stories", not that it happened to Keyser.
5. **No room to stand out.** A flat listing looks like every other one. — V29, V33, V35
6. **A low ceiling** *(hearsay)*. Top Etsy digital sellers reach about six figures a year, the top 1 to 2%.
   — V49. Keyser says this was "heard", not measured.
7. **Copying.** The transcript says Amazon copies products and resells them under its own name.
   — V8. Said of Amazon, not Etsy.
8. **Generic journals and templates** meet massive competition there. — V1, V26, V42

What Keyser says **for** Etsy: one video, from 2022. Etsy's search brings buyers without a
following, and the fees are the price of that traffic; add search keywords to template listings.
— V54

The softer line in some videos is "do not rely only on Etsy", not "do not use it". — V7, V11, V12,
V26, V39

Keyser's own Etsy history is unclear. One video lists Etsy among the things tried since starting in
2014, alongside launches, paid ads, affiliates and Shopify (V48). Another says Keyser and a friend
started setting up an Etsy shop and decided it was not worth it "even before we made our first
listing" (V49). No video gives Keyser's own Etsy sales figures.

The few Etsy-usable tips: write the title from the buyer's pain point, get 5 variations from
ChatGPT, and spend no more than 1 hour on it (V46); describe the result, not the features (V11);
use search keywords (V54).

## Part 6. Where the method does not fit your shop (my reading, not Keyser's)

Each point is marked FACT or INFERENCE.

1. **FACT:** the engine Keyser shows in these videos is long-form YouTube feeding an email list
   (V4, V24, V34, V49); the method itself says one content platform you enjoy (V49, V51).
2. **FACT:** the shop has no email list and no content channel. **INFERENCE:** that is not a reason
   the method cannot fit: Keyser says to build the list from day one (V39) and the audience alongside
   the product (V55, V56), so it is work the method asks for. Putting that work off is my
   prioritisation for an owner with a full-time job, not something Keyser or the shop's numbers
   prove. Meanwhile the one thing Keyser says for Etsy, search traffic without a following (V54),
   is the traffic the shop depends on.
3. **FACT, read 14 Sep 2026 by Windows from Shop Stats:** in the 30 days to 14 Sep the shop had 119
   visits and no orders; 8 visits were in the "Etsy search" category
   (`claude desktoi/ETSY_STATS_2026-09-14.md`, home-notes). The #2 ranking for "dementia caregiver
   binder" (12 Sep) is real, but that phrase had 87 searches in 30 days. GPT-6 Pro (review 35)
   reports from Etsy's Shop Stats glossary that "Etsy search" leaves out searches made in the Etsy
   app, which count under "Etsy app and other Etsy pages" (22 visits). **Confirmed by Windows the
   same day** from the glossary, read signed in: it "doesn't include ... search traffic in the Etsy
   app". So 8 is a floor for search visits, not the total. **INFERENCE:** traffic is low. These figures do not say why,
   whether ads would help, or which listings have had a fair test.
   ~~so the shop is barely being found, which is Keyser's warning in practice~~ Struck the same
   day: it read low traffic as proof of Keyser's reason for it.
   ~~It shows the shop can be seen without ads, so "you must pay to be seen" is not true of this
   shop.~~ Struck 14 Sep: that read one ranking as findability, and the 30-day stats say otherwise.
4. **Reported by Keyser, read by hand in the transcripts, not independently checked:** Keyser earns from the alternative. Keyser is a Systeme.io affiliate, paid when people
   upgrade (V12, V18, V27, V39), and sells a program teaching the YouTube method, Freedom Creator
   Club (V1). That lowers how much weight the Etsy warnings can carry; it is not evidence that they
   are wrong. The fees, reach and policies Keyser describes can be checked directly instead.
   This is in the transcript part of those summaries, not in the extracted steps, so the quote
   check did not cover it; I read it by hand in the summaries.
5. **FACT:** the Etsy numbers are thin. The six-figure ceiling is hearsay (V49), the "like 30" price
   is one remark with no unit (V43), the shutdown stories are second-hand (V7), and no video shows
   Keyser's own Etsy results; V49 says Keyser stopped before a first listing.
6. **FACT that Keyser reports it (not checked):** Keyser's own traffic test: six months of a weekly blog post and 6 to 9 Pinterest pins a
   day brought at best about 60 subscribers a month, hundreds in all, and not one of them bought,
   tracked with Hyros (V28). One test, which argues against copying that exact approach, not that Pinterest cannot work.
7. **Not checked:** Etsy's own rules on sending buyers to an email list or another site. Check them
   before mixing Keyser's funnel with the Etsy shop.
8. **INFERENCE:** the product rules in Part 7 are about the product rather than the channel, and need
   no money to check; whether they raise Etsy sales is untested,
   though they take time.
   The channel advice in Parts 4 and 5 needs an audience the shop does not have yet. Both reviewers
   in review 33, separately, advised using the product rules now and leaving the funnel until a
   product sells on Etsy. That is advice about your time, not a finding about Keyser's method.
   **In practice:** the product rules can be tried on Etsy now; Keyser's full funnel is not available
   to the shop today, and building it is a separate business decision, not part of improving listings.

## Part 7. Keyser's product rules: the checklist for your 47 listings

**When Etsy's own numbers and these rules disagree, the numbers win.** These are Keyser's rules of
thumb for teaching products; both reviewers in review 33 said a product with strong demand should
not be dropped for failing one.

**⛔ Do not rewrite an Etsy title or tags to satisfy R1, R2 or R7.** Titles and tags are how Etsy
buyers find a listing, and they search for what the thing is ("2027 weekly planner A4"), not for a
result. Any result wording belongs early in the description, next to the plain words that say what
the file is (Gemini, reviews 36 and 37; my adaptation, not Keyser's).

**The ten are three different kinds of question** (review 37), and none predicts sales:

- **Wording, read from the listing:** R1, R3, R7. Keyser's lens for teaching products sold from
  Keyser's own page; R1 is the weakest guide on Etsy.
- **The product itself, read from the file:** R4, R5, R6.
- **The market and the shop:** R8, R9, R10. R8 is also Keyser's lens (review 34). R2 is only a
  classification of the buyer's need, not a test.

Use each as a question with a note, not a pass or fail.

**Only four of the ten can be answered by reading the listing.** The rest need a search of rival
listings, the product file itself, or the shop's own stats, and the last column says which.
"Stated" means Keyser says it in those videos; "my reading" means I turned advice into a test.

## Part 8. Mistakes Keyser names

- Building from your own assumptions, or because it would be fun to make, without asking buyers
  first. — V1, V4, V5, V7, V29, V46, V51
- Reselling low-effort Canva templates or information anyone can find with a search or an AI
  tool. — V5, V26
- Spending time on logos, brand colours or t-shirts before any sales. — V15
- Jumping between niches and business models instead of sticking with one. — V24, V25
- Spending months or years building a following before making a product. — V55, V56
- Believing you need complex funnels or piles of daily listings. — V27

## Where the advice disagrees with itself

- **Wait or sell now.** Survey at 100 to 200 subscribers before building (V12, V36), against: do not
  wait for any subscriber count before selling (V27), and start selling workshops at about 100 (V43).
- **Build or pre-sell.** Make it, then sell it (V1, V17), against: sell it, then make it (V3, V11).
- **Upsells.** Add them (V7, V15), against: not as a beginner (V20).
- **Etsy.** Warnings or cautions about it (42 videos), against: do not rely only on it (V7, V11, V12, V26, V39),
  against: use its search traffic (V54, 2022).
- **Etsy history.** Tried Etsy (V48), against: stopped before the first listing (V49).
- **Ebook price.** $59 for the first ebook (V2, V12, V23), against: $49 for the same ebook and
  "around $29" for an ebook in general (V37).
